**Gate reasons**: total=13 >= 9 ## Research Question Can a MAPE-K controller that explicitly models heterogeneous requirements—mixing hard thresholds such as safety/cost limits with optimization goals such as task quality and latency—improve adaptation decisions in AI agent workflows compared with single-objective or fixed-policy orchestration? ## Scores | Dimension | Score (0-3) | |---|---| | significance | 3 | | novelty | 2 | | differentiation | 3 | | feasibility | 2 | | falsifiability | 3 | | **total** | **13 / 15** | ## Innovation Claim - **Problem gap**: Existing methods fail because they typically optimize a single utility signal or use fixed orchestration policies, so they do not explicitly reconcile hard constraints such as safety and cost limits with soft optimization goals such as quality and latency during adaptation. - **Our delta**: We propose a MAPE-K controller for AI agent workflows that explicitly models heterogeneous requirements by separating hard threshold constraints from optimization objectives and using that structure to drive adaptation decisions under uncertainty. - **Why it matters**: Which improves overall workflow utility and reduces unnecessary adaptations under changing workloads, uncertainty, and multi-constraint operating conditions. ## Falsification Design - **Hypothesis**: In dynamic multi-agent workflow settings with shifting workload and uncertainty, a heterogeneous-goal MAPE-K controller yields higher constrained utility and fewer unnecessary adaptations than a single-objective utility-based orchestrator. - **Baseline**: Single-objective adaptive orchestrator that optimizes a weighted-sum utility over quality, latency, and cost without explicit hard-vs-soft requirement modeling. - **Metric**: Primary: constrained utility = task-quality score minus latency/cost penalties with zero credit for any run violating hard safety or budget thresholds; Secondary: adaptation churn rate per 100 tasks. - **Failure condition**: Constrained utility improvement < 5% over baseline or adaptation churn reduction < 10%, or any statistically significant increase in hard-constraint violation rate. - **Expected signal**: At least 8% higher constrained utility and 15% lower adaptation churn while maintaining equal or lower hard-constraint violations. -
