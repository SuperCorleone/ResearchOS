# Research Brief: Meta-Learned Model Selection for Heterogeneous-Goal Self-Adaptive AI Systems - **Proposal ID**: `meta_learned_model_selection_f` - **Direction**: self-adaptive-systems - **Gate**: `pass` (total=13/15, repeat_degree=`low`) - **Gate reasons**: total=13 >= 9 ## Research Question Can replacing Bayesian Model Averaging in the analyze stage of a MAPE-K loop with a stacking-based meta-learner improve prediction accuracy and downstream adaptation quality when self-adaptive AI systems must satisfy heterogeneous goals such as thresholds and optimization objectives? ## Scores | Dimension | Score (0-3) | |---|---| | significance | 3 | | novelty | 2 | | differentiation | 3 | | feasibility | 2 | | falsifiability | 3 | | **total** | **13 / 15** | ## Innovation Claim - **Problem gap**: Existing methods fail because Bayesian Model Averaging assumes the true model is among the candidates and uses posterior-based weighting that may not adapt well when heterogeneous predictive models vary in usefulness across contexts and mixed requirement types. - **Our delta**: We propose replacing the BMA-based analyze step in the MAPE-K loop with a stacking-based meta-learner that learns context-dependent combinations of heterogeneous base models from observed predictive performance. - **Why it matters**: Which improves prediction accuracy and adaptation effectiveness under heterogeneous self-adaptive system goals involving both threshold satisfaction and optimization objectives. ## Falsification Design - **Hypothesis**: In self-adaptive AI systems with heterogeneous goals, a stacking-based analyze module yields higher predictive accuracy and better downstream adaptation quality than Bayesian Model Averaging across scenarios where model superiority changes with context. - **Baseline**: MAPE-K analyze module using Bayesian Model Averaging over the same candidate model set. - **Metric**: Primary: downstream adaptation utility aggregated over threshold-satisfaction rate and optimization regret; Secondary: predictive error reduction (e.g., RMSE or log-loss depending on target). - **Failure condition**: Disprove if stacking improves aggregate adaptation utility by less than 2% over BMA or fails to outperform BMA on predictive error in a statistically significant way across evaluation scenarios. -
