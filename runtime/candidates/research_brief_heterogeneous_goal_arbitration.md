# Research Brief: Heterogeneous-Goal Arbitration for Self-Adaptive Agent Workflows - **Proposal ID**: `heterogeneous_goal_arbitration` - **Direction**: agentic-workflow-optimization - **Gate**: `pass` (total=12/15, repeat_degree=`low`) - **Gate reasons**: total=12 >= 9 ## Research Question Can a self-adaptive controller that explicitly arbitrates between threshold-style constraints (e.g., cost, latency, safety) and optimization goals (e.g., quality, task success) improve multi-agent workflow performance over static orchestration policies under changing task conditions? ## Scores | Dimension | Score (0-3) | |---|---| | significance | 3 | | novelty | 2 | | differentiation | 2 | | feasibility | 2 | | falsifiability | 3 | | **total** | **12 / 15** | ## Innovation Claim - **Problem gap**: Existing methods fail because they either optimize a single objective or adapt without explicitly arbitrating between hard threshold constraints and soft optimization goals when task conditions change over time. - **Our delta**: We propose a MAPE-K-style self-adaptive orchestration layer that explicitly reasons over heterogeneous goals, prioritizing threshold-style constraints such as cost, latency, and safety while optimizing quality and task success among feasible options. - **Why it matters**: Which improves overall workflow utility and reduces unnecessary replanning under shifting task conditions and changing objective trade-offs in multi-agent workflows. ## Falsification Design - **Hypothesis**: A heterogeneous-goal self-adaptive controller achieves higher constrained utility than a static orchestration policy and a single-objective adaptive controller, while performing fewer unnecessary replans, under nonstationary task conditions. - **Baseline**: Best fixed static orchestration policy selected on validation data, plus a single-objective adaptive controller that replans to maximize expected quality only. - **Metric**: Primary: constrained utility = task success/quality score minus penalties for violating latency, cost, or safety thresholds; Secondary: replan rate per 100 tasks and threshold-violation rate. - **Failure condition**: Constrained utility improvement is < 5% over the better baseline or threshold-violation rate is not reduced, and replan rate is not reduced by at least 10% under changing conditions. -
