# Research Brief: Heterogeneous-Goal Model Uncertainty for Self-Adaptive MAPE-K Security Loops - **Proposal ID**: `heterogeneous_goal_model_uncer` - **Direction**: self-adaptive-systems - **Gate**: `pass` (total=14/15, repeat_degree=`low`) - **Gate reasons**: total=14 >= 9 ## Research Question Can Bayesian model averaging over multiple candidate environment and threat models improve adaptation decisions in self-adaptive security systems when the MAPE-K loop must optimize heterogeneous goals such as protection, latency, and cost? ## Scores | Dimension | Score (0-3) | |---|---| | significance | 3 | | novelty | 3 | | differentiation | 3 | | feasibility | 2 | | falsifiability | 3 | | **total** | **14 / 15** | ## Innovation Claim - **Problem gap**: Existing methods fail because they handle model uncertainty in self-adaptive systems only in limited domains and do not jointly reason over heterogeneous goals such as security protection, latency, and cost when choosing adaptations. - **Our delta**: We propose a Bayesian model-averaging adaptation mechanism for security-oriented MAPE-K loops that explicitly evaluates multiple candidate environment and threat models against heterogeneous threshold and optimization goals. - **Why it matters**: Which improves adaptation decision quality and reduces unnecessary or maladaptive security reconfigurations under shifting operating conditions and uncertain threat dynamics. ## Falsification Design - **Hypothesis**: In a security self-adaptive MAPE-K system facing non-stationary workload and threat conditions, Bayesian model averaging over multiple candidate environment/threat models with heterogeneous-goal decision logic yields higher utility and fewer unnecessary reconfigurations than a single-model expected-utility baseline. - **Baseline**: Single best-model MAPE-K controller using one fixed environment/threat model and a conventional weighted-sum utility function for protection, latency, and cost. - **Metric**: Primary: average normalized multi-objective utility across protection, latency, and cost over time; Secondary: rate of unnecessary reconfigurations per 100 decision intervals. - **Failure condition**: Normalized utility improvement is < 2% and unnecessary reconfiguration reduction is < 5%, or either metric is worse than baseline under distribution shift scenarios. - **Expected signal**: At least 5% higher normalized utility and 10% fewer unnecessary reconfigurations under shifting conditions. -
